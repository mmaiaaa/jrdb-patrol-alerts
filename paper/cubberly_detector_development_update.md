# Cubberly detector and visibility audit (development draft)

## Scope and observations

The moving JRDB 2022 recording `cubberly-auditorium-2019-04-22_0` is assigned to development only. The initial raw-image run examined frames `000070`–`000149`, with all five 752 × 480 native cameras, for 400 camera images. The detector settings, weight hash and environment are documented in `paper/cubberly_detection_pilot.md`; predictions are kept in `outputs/local/cubberly_native_pilot.jsonl` and were not generated using ground-truth labels. The cache has all five entries for each of the 80 frames. Box totals for cameras 0/2/4/6/8 are 245/0/444/449/171; zero-box frame counts are 0/80/0/0/18. These are detections, not unique people or recall.

Four supplied camera-2 contact sheets cover **all 80** frames. At contact-sheet resolution they show a largely continuous view of an interior wall while the robot's viewpoint shifts; no clearly visible person appears in that view. The camera-2 zero count is thus consistent with the scene shown in this *particular short interval*, without establishing detector sensitivity. A small person, reflection, occlusion or partial figure cannot be ruled out without full-resolution inspection. The other views can contain people at the same instant, so scene-level person presence cannot be inferred from camera 2 alone.

The supplied contact sheet for the 18 zero-box camera-8 frames covers `000070`–`000087`. At its end, especially `000086`–`000087`, a partial figure appears at the right image edge; `000085` is worth inspecting at original resolution. Frame `000087` in the independently provided five-view sheet also shows a partially visible figure in camera 8 and zero boxes there, while people in cameras 0, 4 and 6 have boxes. This is **a possible missed, truncated person in camera 8**, not a measured false negative: define person visibility, minimum area and annotation eligibility before scoring. Inspect those original JPEGs and, if available, relevant source annotations. It is not justified to label all 18 zero-box frames person-free.

The previously reported same-camera IoU ≥ 0.5 counts (39/0/88/68/41 box **pairs** for cameras 0/2/4/6/8) merit a separate crowded-view review. Overlapping boxes can belong to the same person, distinct occluding people or false detections. They are not a duplicate-person rate.

## Development next step

Finish the raw-image cache over the **full 1,296-frame Cubberly development recording**, preserving the pilot output under its existing name. Use the same weights and settings, explicitly setting `--start-frame 0 --max-frames 0` and a new local output path. Audit that the full cache has 1,296 frame IDs and 6,480 frame/camera rows, unchanged model hash and no duplicate or missing views. Inspect representative early/middle/late five-camera frames using the existing `review_jrdb_native_detections.py`, and inspect any visually suspicious zero-box frames at original resolution. This broadens detector coverage *within development*; it is not a held-out evaluation.

Before producing any event metric, validate per-camera LiDAR projection and person-range association using measured sensor points (without reference 3D boxes as inference input); check timing or report frame-index outcomes only. Separately adjudicate development event candidates and negative intervals, with explicit censoring and identity rules. Freeze the reference policy, model choice, baselines, group split and thresholds before inspecting final-group outcomes. Only then compute person/encounter detection, false alerts per verified exposure and alert latency with independent-recording uncertainty. The inspected contact sheets are quality-control evidence, not reference labels or security-intrusion outcomes.

The RTX 2060 completed the 400-image sequential pilot. That establishes feasibility of this small inference task; the first image dominated the cold timing, and sustained multi-stage or onboard latency has not been measured. There is no evidence here that a new computer is required.
