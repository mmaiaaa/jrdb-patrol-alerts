# Five-camera Cubberly registration review (development draft)

The five-camera diagnostic processed four development frames (`000087`, `000432`, `000864`, `001295`), giving 20 native-image views. Its full cached prediction file has SHA-256 `2e13fa212e647561765f0cc203ec60fd658e6377c6a7c655200700beddc56a16` and its `lidars.yaml` has SHA-256 `ce76679a029bde9b934eaa8781273c3accbf7871ced47294940e79ed92a4ed01`. The source cloud has 16,112–19,287 declared points in these selected frames; 3,277–5,926 project inside an individual native camera depending on frame/view. These counts test processing coverage, not pixel-registration accuracy.

Qualitative inspection of the **ten uploaded views at frames `000432` and `001295`** shows coherent upper-LiDAR scan patterns on room surfaces in cameras 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8. Frame `000432` camera 2 includes two nearby people and a column; returns project in and around the detections, but columns, walls and floor also contribute points. At frame `001295` camera 0, dense near-range returns appear over two foreground people. Camera 2 at that frame also has near points on a large pillar as well as boxes elsewhere, illustrating why selecting the nearest point inside a rectangle could attribute a pillar to a person. Several crowded views contain overlapping or low-confidence boxes. Visual coherence at these two time points does **not** establish a 3D person match, numerical reprojection residual, precise sensor synchronization, or general accuracy across 1,296 frames.

The figures at frame `001295` camera 8 contain no detector box while the camera still has projected scene points. An absent box is not proof of a person-free 3D sector. The colored points use the upper-LiDAR XY radius (yellow ≤3 m, cyan ≤10 m, pink farther); the white rectangles are unverified 2D detections. Background points in a rectangle and unmatched points outside it must be kept distinct from measured person range.

The next development check is `scripts/audit_jrdb_box_depth_support.py`. It reads only raw upper clouds, camera calibration and cached detector boxes. For each box on four development frames, it reports counts and half-meter upper-LiDAR radial bins for points that project inside an inner box region, plus counts inside the whole box. It **does not** select a closest bin, infer foreground, or generate an alert. Boxes with no inner points require an explicit unknown-distance policy; boxes with returns at several radial bins require foreground/background separation before any person-range candidate is credible. Examine a sample of these cases on the overlay PNGs and at original resolution.

Before publishing a performance result: (1) check calibration and camera/LiDAR time offsets independently; (2) define foreground association, occlusion and cross-camera deduplication without access to reference labels at runtime; (3) measure range error on development examples with known 3D positions and human-verified association; (4) independently adjudicate encounter boundaries and actual negative exposure; (5) freeze baselines and group splits before held-out scoring. Until then, the result is a reproducible raw-sensor feasibility study on **one moving development recording**, not a validated proximity-alert system.

The [original JRDB paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11792v4) describes separate `timestamps/<sequence>/frames_img.json` and `frames_pc.json` files. The four JRDB 2022 archives inspected so far contain no timestamp entries; their absence there is not evidence the files are unavailable elsewhere. Check the account's [official dataset downloads](https://jrdb.erc.monash.edu/) for the corresponding timing data before claiming sensor synchronization or delay in seconds. The official site also states that test annotations were released in September 2025. This may permit a stronger external holdout later, subject to access and compatible annotation policy; its labels must stay closed until the analysis protocol is fixed.
