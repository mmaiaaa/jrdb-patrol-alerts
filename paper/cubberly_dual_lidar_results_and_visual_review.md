# Cubberly dual-LiDAR geometry results and visual review (development draft)

The four-frame diagnostic used the development-only Cubberly 3D label file SHA-256 `c3a90751e2731360c8dd827d76a09f5aff11a503f2198460badaf74a91ff26bf` and `lidars.yaml` SHA-256 `ce76679a029bde9b934eaa8781273c3accbf7871ced47294940e79ed92a4ed01`. It audited **114** pedestrian boxes across frames `000087`, `000432`, `000864`, `001295`; **13** were evaluable and had exploratory XY box-center radii ≤3 m. All 13 had one or more **combined upper and lower** returns under both tested label-frame hypotheses. This checks point occupancy within *annotated* 3D boxes; it is neither person detection sensitivity nor proof of foreground association.

In frame `000432`, `pedestrian:33` explains the single-sensor gap: within the 3D box, the upper cloud contributes **0** points under either hypothesis; the transformed lower cloud contributes **479** points under the upper-frame hypothesis and **497** under the ego-frame hypothesis. JRDB reports `num_points=497` for this label. This agreement is useful for debugging, but it cannot by itself prove the annotation frame. Under the same frame, `pedestrian:34` has **109** combined points and reported `num_points=109` under either hypothesis; `pedestrian:35` has **1,590** combined points and reported `num_points=1,590` under either hypothesis. These examples illustrate why matching a reported count can be inconclusive. Other near cases differ considerably: frame `001295`, `pedestrian:104` reports **2,761** points, versus **1,635** under the upper-frame hypothesis and **1,702** under the ego-frame hypothesis.

Across **110 boxes with positive reported `num_points`** in the supplied CSV, combined counts are numerically closer to reported values for 93 boxes under the ego-frame hypothesis, nine under the upper-frame hypothesis, and eight tie (absolute point-count difference). This is an exploratory comparison, **not a calibration score or validated frame identification**. Different sensor preprocessing, annotation provenance, 3D box orientation conventions and occlusion can affect these differences. The original [JRDB paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11792v4) states that its labels were annotated on merged upper/lower clouds and describes the 3D boxes as centered cuboids with `rot_z` rotation. This alone does not establish how the **JRDB 2022** archive encoded all count and coordinate details.

`scripts/visualize_jrdb_dev_3d_box_frame_hypotheses.py` generates a four-frame contact sheet using five *raw native images* per frame. The top row projects nearby evaluable label centers and assumed 3D box edges as if labels are expressed in upper-LiDAR coordinates; the bottom row treats labels as ego-frame coordinates and converts to upper before projecting. Box edges use **assumed** local X length, local Y width and yaw sign. Points and detector boxes are not drawn. Look especially at `000432` label `33` and at the crowded `000864` and `001295` examples: is a projected center on a visible person in the expected view, or on a wall/pillar/background? Do not interpret missing projection as an invisible pedestrian without inspecting view geometry and label status.

The sheet is a **manual diagnostic**, not an automatic validator. The same underlying 3D reference boxes generated the comparison; visual appearance is independent evidence only to the extent a reviewer verifies persons in the raw image and timing. Review image/LiDAR acquisition timestamps separately, then human-check ID association and range on development frames before selecting any online algorithm. Keep reference annotations out of runtime and unopened sequence(s) out of development.
