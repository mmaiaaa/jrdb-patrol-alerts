# Cubberly development: stratified 2D / 3D projection audit

**Evidence status: exploratory, development-only annotation agreement.** The run covers 1,296 frames, 3,155 evaluable 3D-near frame-track records and 4,516 matched 3D/2D frame-camera-track records; 4,457 have 2D `no_eval=false`. The annotation-ego hypothesis places 3,826 projected 3D centers inside the corresponding same-ID 2D box (85.84%); the annotation-upper hypothesis places 3,470 inside (77.86%). Paired outcomes: 3,299 inside under both, 527 under ego only, 171 under upper only and 460 under neither. These repeated and correlated annotations cannot be treated as independent detector or calibration measurements.

The conservative **one-pixel** edge definition classifies 2,450 evaluable boxes as interior: ego fits 2,437 (99.47%), upper fits 2,173 (88.69%). Of the 13 interior ego misses, review-queue examples include small disagreements of 0.04 to 10.56 pixels, severe/full occlusion, and boxes whose left edge is only 1.06 to 1.21 pixels from the image edge. Thus *interior by this rule* is not the same as safely away from truncation. The remaining 2,007 edge-touching pairs yield 1,389 ego-inside and 1,297 upper-inside; 567 ego-projected centers lie outside the corresponding image. The edge rule is a diagnostic threshold, not a source annotation of visibility; analyze sensitivity to wider margins and visually check individual cases before using this agreement to fix a coordinate convention.

Same-ID 2D annotations appear in at least one native view for 3,131 of 3,155 evaluable 3D-near frame-track records; 24 have no same-ID 2D annotation. Of those 3,131 *annotated* records, ego centers fall inside at least one native 2D box for 3,123, compared with 2,863 under upper. Neither same-ID coverage nor projected center containment verifies that a human is actually visible or correctly detected. Across 35 annotated tracks, 24 favor ego on within-2D-box rate, eight favor upper and three tie. Every camera separately favors ego on the same frame-camera diagnostic, but frames and cameras from one recording are dependent.

The next immediate step is human inspection of the **13** top-priority evaluable, nominally nonedge ego misses. `scripts/visualize_jrdb_reprojection_priority.py` builds previous/current/next full-resolution native image sheets with the dataset's 2D box and the two projected reference centers annotated *only* on the middle image. It writes a blank human-review template. Reviewers should record whether the box describes the same person, whether the center is near an image edge or another person, degree of occlusion, whether the projection overlays plausible anatomy, and whether the case is unresolved. Inspect sampled edge-touching disagreements afterward. A three-image sheet does not establish exact image/LiDAR acquisition times, and annotation agreement does not quantify foreground LiDAR range.

The original [JRDB annotation paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11792v4) says 2D boxes were initialized by projecting 3D boxes and then manually refined. Accordingly, matching the two references is correlated evidence. No reference label or review result may enter online detection, range estimation or alerts. The next separate experiment must use raw camera detections plus raw LiDAR for a causal person-range estimator, with confidence/unknown outputs and offline reference checks. Verification of original timestamps and independent held-out event evaluation remain required for performance claims.
