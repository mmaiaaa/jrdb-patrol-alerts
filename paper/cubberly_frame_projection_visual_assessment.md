# Cubberly 3D annotation frame: four-frame visual assessment (development draft)

Four contact sheets compare projected JRDB 3D boxes under two hypothetical annotation frames using five original camera images per frame. Top-row **yellow** assumes annotations are in the upper Velodyne frame; bottom-row **cyan** assumes annotations are in the ego frame, converted with `upper2ego` before using `sensor_<camera>.upper2cam`. The sheets read only development labels and raw images. The 3D cuboid code also assumes local-X length, local-Y width, geometric center and `rot_z` yaw. Both alternatives use the same 3D label coordinates, and cropping a cuboid at the camera boundary can show only a line or face.

In frame `000087`, the sole near evaluable label (`pedestrian:6`) projects into camera 6 under both alternatives. The cyan cuboid more closely encloses the dark foreground person; the yellow outline appears mostly as a vertical line and floor fragment. At frame `000432`, the six near labels generate more person-shaped cyan cuboids in several cameras, while several yellow edges run through floors or across people. The scene is crowded, so a geometrically plausible cuboid can still be assigned to a different person. At `000864`, both rows have cropped outlines in some views and we cannot adjudicate each track visually from the composite alone. At `001295`, two cyan cuboids cover most of the height of nearby people in camera 0; the corresponding yellow outlines appear largely near their feet. The contact sheets **favor the ego-frame hypothesis qualitatively in these selected views**. Neither the count of projected centers inside an image nor this unblinded visual comparison is a quantitative alignment score, timestamp check or proof of the correct 3D frame for all JRDB 2022 scenes.

The four-frame dual-LiDAR occupancy audit found 13/13 near evaluable boxes with merged upper/lower point support under **both** assumptions. For 110 boxes with positive `num_points`, combined raw counts were numerically closer to reported counts for 93 boxes under the ego hypothesis, nine under upper and eight tied. Since the annotation counts and raw clouds may differ in filtering, and the boxes/angles are themselves assumptions, those values should not be used as a formal frame-selection statistic.

`scripts/inspect_jrdb_dev_native_2d_reference.py` next inspects the sequence's *native-camera pedestrian labels* inside the already downloaded `labels.zip`. It reports the actual 2D schema and selected frame/track-ID overlap with the development 3D labels. Only after its schema is verified should a separate offline tool compare each projected 3D box with the **same-track native 2D box**, using a documented reprojection error and occlusion policy. The [JRDB paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11792v4) says the 2D labels were initialized by projection of the 3D annotations and then manually refined, so such a check is useful but **not fully independent external validation**. The coordinate convention must still be established against source documentation and representative visual review. No 2D or 3D labels may enter online detection, range estimation or alert triggering.
