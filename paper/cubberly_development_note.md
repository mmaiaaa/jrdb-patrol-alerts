# Moving-recording feasibility: Cubberly (development draft)

This section records a feasibility audit, not an evaluation of the proposed alert system. The `cubberly-auditorium-2019-04-22_0` recording is identified as a moving-robot example in the [original JRDB paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.11792v4). The locally downloaded JRDB 2022 training archives contained 1,296 corresponding frame IDs in the stitched and camera-0 images and both upper/lower point-cloud streams. The 3D label frame IDs matched. Matching IDs alone cannot verify capture timestamps, metric box coordinates, or projection quality.

Using a preliminary horizontal box-center radius of 3 m, development-only labels generated 37 contiguous person-near runs. Each starts immediately after an evaluable far observation for the same person ID; 34 end immediately before an evaluable far observation and three reach the end of the recording. This is evidence of possible boundary transitions, subject to annotation quality, geometry, and an eventual duration/gap policy. It does not establish 37 independently verified encounters. The 238 frames without an evaluable person within that radius form only **three contiguous intervals**; they are not 238 independent negatives or an exposure in seconds. These values were produced by `scripts/audit_jrdb_dev_boundaries.py` on this development recording and are provisional pending human review.

Before reference construction, independently inspect run boundaries and the three putative negative intervals, record ambiguous or interpolated observations, verify the label and LiDAR frame conventions, and establish acquisition timing. In a separate diagnostic, `scripts/audit_jrdb_cubberly_projection.py` projects raw upper-LiDAR points into native camera-0 images using `sensor_0.upper2cam`, `sensor_0.distorted_img_K`, and `sensor_0.D` from the downloaded calibration. The preview colors indicate horizontal range **from the upper LiDAR**, not the robot-relative person-center radius used to create candidates. These drawings test visual registration only; they neither validate camera coverage of every person nor supply range estimates to an online predictor. The [official JRDB toolkit](https://github.com/JRDB-dataset/jrdb_toolkit) documents distinct LiDAR and camera coordinate systems. Report any observed misalignment as a result requiring investigation, without silently adjusting calibration to favor the proposed method.

**Unfinished evidence:** source timestamps or an explicitly justified frame-time approximation; human adjudication; raw-sensor detector/tracker predictions and LiDAR-based range estimation; independent validation and untouched testing; stronger conventional alert baselines; uncertainty intervals over independent recording groups. No false-alert, recall, latency, or deployment claim is supported by these development counts.
